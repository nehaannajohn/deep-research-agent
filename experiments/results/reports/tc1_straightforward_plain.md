# Research Report
**Query:** What are the real-world risks and benefits of using synthetic data to train or fine-tune large language models? Focus on data quality, bias, and evaluation.

## Summary
Using synthetic data to train or fine-tune large language models (LLMs) has both benefits and risks. The benefits include improved model performance, reduced bias, and faster training. However, there are risks associated with data quality, bias, and evaluation.

## Benefits
Using synthetic data can enhance model performance and reduce bias when fine-tuning LLMs [c0]. It can be particularly effective when training data is scarce [c1]. Pre-training on a mixture of natural web texts and rephrased synthetic data can speed up training 5-10 times at larger data budgets [c2]. Synthetic data can also improve the downstream performance of smaller models when generated via fine-tuned teacher LLMs [c3].

## Risks
However, using synthetic data without proper care can cause unintended effects, such as 'model collapse' [c6]. Synthetic data training can produce undesirable outcomes, including a decrease in linguistic diversity [c7]. Synthetic data often contains repetitive and structurally predictable elements, simplifying the complexity of real data [c8]. Training on synthetic data can lead to overfitting, reducing the model's ability to deal with real-world variability [c10].

## Data Quality and Bias
The quality of synthetic data is crucial, as low-quality data can negatively impact model performance [c11]. Moreover, synthetic data generated by biased LLMs can propagate and amplify existing biases [c13]. Models trained on synthetic data can inherit similar preferences and biases from the models that generated the data [c14]. Fine-tuning with synthetic data generated by a single source model can increase self-preference bias in LLMs [c15].

## Evaluation
To evaluate the performance of LLMs trained or fine-tuned on synthetic data, various metrics can be used, including automated metrics [c19], pass rate [c20], MultiNLI and HANS test sets [c21], token-level AD and pass@1 [c22], LLM-as-a-Judge and Reward Models [c23], perplexity of response, and instance diversity [c24]. Statistical methods, such as bootstrapping with 1,000 samples [c26] and paired t-tests with Bonferroni correction [c27], can be used to compute confidence intervals and test significance.

## Mitigating Risks
To mitigate the risks associated with using synthetic data, diverse sources can be used to generate synthetic data [c18]. Certain strategies can be employed to generate synthetic data that mitigates biases in LLMs [c16]. Removing low-quality synthetic data from model training sets can also improve model performance [c11].
