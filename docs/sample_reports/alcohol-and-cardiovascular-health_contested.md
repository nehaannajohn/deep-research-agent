# Research Report
**Query:** Does moderate alcohol consumption provide cardiovascular health benefits or does any amount of alcohol increase mortality risk?

## Summary
The relationship between alcohol consumption and cardiovascular health is complex, with conflicting evidence on whether moderate drinking provides health benefits or increases mortality risk.

## Cardiovascular Health Benefits
Several studies suggest that moderate alcohol consumption is associated with a lower risk of cardiovascular disease (CVD) mortality [c0][c1][c2][c4][c5][c6]. Moderate drinking may help raise levels of 'good' HDL cholesterol [c3]. Specifically, alcohol consumption at 2.5–14.9 g/day was associated with a 14–25% reduction in the risk of all outcomes assessed compared with abstaining from alcohol [c5].

## Contrasting Views on Mortality Risk
However, there are conflicting findings on the impact of alcohol consumption on mortality risk. One source indicates that moderate alcohol consumption is associated with a lower risk of CVD mortality [c0], while others suggest that any amount of alcohol consumption increases mortality risk [c7], with genetic evidence supporting a linear increase in the risk of premature death with increasing alcohol consumption [c11][c13]. Similarly, another study found an association between moderate alcohol consumption and lower risk of MI, total stroke, and CVD mortality [c1], which is contradicted by the findings that any amount of alcohol consumption increases mortality risk [c7][c11][c13].

## Reconciling the Evidence
The contested findings can be summarized as follows:
- One source indicates that moderate alcohol consumption is associated with a lower risk of CVD mortality [c0], while others indicate that any amount of alcohol consumption increases mortality risk [c7][c11][c13].
- Moderate alcohol consumption is associated with a lower risk of MI, total stroke, and CVD mortality [c1], while others suggest that any amount of alcohol consumption increases mortality risk [c7][c11][c13].
- Some studies show an association between moderate alcohol intake and a lower risk of dying from heart disease [c2], which is disputed by the claim that any amount of alcohol consumption increases mortality risk [c7].
- Light to moderate alcohol consumption is associated with a reduced risk of multiple cardiovascular outcomes [c4][c6], but one review reported monotonically increased risk with greater consumption [c13].
- Alcohol consumption at low levels was associated with a reduced risk of all outcomes assessed [c5], but this is contradicted by the findings that any amount of alcohol consumption increases mortality risk [c7][c11][c13] and that there is no significant net benefit of alcohol consumption on all-cause mortality at any age [c8].
- Low-volume drinkers had nonsignificant relative risk estimates compared with abstainers in a fully adjusted model [c14], which contrasts with the claim that any amount of alcohol consumption increases mortality risk [c7].

## Conclusion
The evidence is mixed, and it is unclear whether moderate alcohol consumption provides cardiovascular health benefits or increases mortality risk. While some studies suggest that moderate drinking is associated with a lower risk of CVD mortality, others indicate that any amount of alcohol consumption increases mortality risk.
